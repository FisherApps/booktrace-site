The real question

Did you finish the book—or do you want to remember how you listened?

For many readers, a title, shelf, and rating are enough. For an audiobook listener, the performance and time are part of the book. The right tracker depends on which record you want to keep.

Where Goodreads still fits

Choose social discovery when that is what you want.

Goodreads is useful for public shelves, ratings, reviews, recommendations, and seeing what friends read. Booktrace is not trying to recreate that network.

If conversation and discovery matter most, a social reading platform may be the right centre of your reading life.

Where Booktrace fits

Questions, answered

Before changing how you track audiobooks.

Can Goodreads track audiobooks?

It can catalogue audiobook editions and reading status. A dedicated tracker adds listening-specific details such as chapters, narrators, speed, sessions, and actual time.

Is Booktrace a social network?

No. It is a private iPhone tracker with no public profile and no Booktrace account.

Can I use both?

Yes. Keep a public or cross-format catalogue elsewhere and use Booktrace for the detailed listening record.

Does Booktrace import Goodreads?

No. Booktrace does not currently claim an automatic Goodreads import. You choose which audiobooks become part of your Booktrace library.

Can it combine Audible and Libby?

Yes. Add books from any player to one Booktrace library. Booktrace does not access or import the private account history inside those services.

Does it remember narrators?

Yes. Narrators are part of the audiobook record and can be browsed across your library.
