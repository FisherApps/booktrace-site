Booktrace/About

Why Booktrace exists

Every book deserves to be counted properly.

Booktrace is an independent iPhone app built around a simple idea: a paperback, an ebook, and an audiobook are all reading, but each moves differently. Pages for print and ebooks. Hours, chapters, voices, and speeds for audio. One library for all of it.

The missing record

Your books live everywhere. Booktrace remembers all of them.

A reading life moves between a paperback on the nightstand, an ebook in Kindle or Libby, and an audiobook in Audible, Spotify, or Apple Books. Each app can show what is happening inside itself. Booktrace exists to give the reader one lasting record across all of them.

It does not replace those apps or quietly pull data from their accounts. You choose what to record: the book, page, chapter, narrator, session, note, rating, or shelf that matters—or bring a Goodreads or Libby export in one import.

Product principles

The decisions behind the app.

Every format is real reading.

A paperback, an ebook, and an audiobook all count. For audio, the narrator, performance, playback speed, and time spent listening belong in the record too.

Private should be the default.

There is no Booktrace account or public profile. The library, history, notes, and ratings stay on the iPhone.

Progress should mean something.

A percentage alone is not enough. Booktrace keeps the page you are on and pages left for print and ebooks, and the chapter, published runtime, personal pace, and time remaining for audiobooks.

The record should feel worth keeping.

Covers, sessions, calendars, stats, narrators, notes, and shelves turn a list of finished titles into a reading history.

A companion, not another reader or player

What Booktrace keeps—and what it leaves alone.

Keep the parts an app forgets

Booktrace is for the durable record around a book: the page or chapter where you stopped, the time it took, the narrator who made a performance memorable, the speed you actually used, the sessions that got you there, and the notes or rating you want to keep.

Read or listen wherever the book lives

People move between print, Kindle, Apple Books, Libby, Audible, Spotify, Everand, and the library. Booktrace does not ask you to replace any of them or pretend to sync private histories it cannot access.

Choose what becomes part of your history

Add a book, choose its format, record a live session, or log one afterward. You decide which pages, chapters, times, notes, quotes, ratings, shelves, and finished books are worth preserving.

Privacy is part of the product

There is no Booktrace account and no public reading profile. Your library and reading history stay on your iPhone. AI discussion is optional and clearly disclosed when the conversation and current position are sent to an AI provider.

Questions about Booktrace

A few straightforward answers.

What is Booktrace?

Booktrace is a book tracker for iPhone that works for print books, ebooks, and audiobooks. Print and ebooks track by page; audiobooks by chapter, narrator, and speed. Sessions, notes, ratings, shelves, goals, and stats cover every format.

Does it replace my ebook app or audiobook player?

No. Keep reading on paper, in Kindle, Apple Books, or Libby, and keep listening in Audible, Spotify, Everand, or another player. Booktrace keeps the history around all of it.

Do I need a Booktrace account?

No. Your library, sessions, notes, and ratings stay on your iPhone. Optional AI discussion is the only feature that sends conversation context to an outside provider.
