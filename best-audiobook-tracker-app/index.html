Best for mixed-format statistics

StoryGraph

Choose a cross-format tracker such as StoryGraph when print books, ebooks, and audiobooks should all feed the same reading goals, charts, and recommendations.

  • Useful for readers moving between formats
  • Broader reading record rather than audiobook depth
  • Community and discovery features
Best for a large public book network

Goodreads

Choose Goodreads when public reviews, familiar shelves, friend activity, and its established catalogue matter more than detailed listening sessions.

  • Large, familiar book community
  • Audiobook editions can be catalogued
  • Not built around listening time or sessions
Best for social reading and book clubs

Fable

Choose Fable when book clubs, community discussion, reading goals, and a social experience are central to why you track books.

  • Social and community-led
  • Useful across reading formats
  • Different purpose from a private listening log

For serious audiobook listeners

Ask whether the app understands time.

A percentage says where you are. It does not say how long the published book is, how quickly you listen, how many hours you actually spent, or which sessions moved you forward.

If those questions matter, look for runtime, speed, chapter, and session tracking—not merely an audiobook label.

For multi-format readers

Ask whether audio should stand apart.

If you mostly want one annual book count across print, ebook, and audio, a general reading tracker may be simpler. If listening is a distinct part of your reading life, audiobook-specific detail becomes more valuable.

There is no reason you cannot use both: one public or cross-format catalogue and one detailed listening record.

Questions, answered

Choosing an audiobook tracker.

What is the best audiobook tracker?

For detailed iPhone listening history, Booktrace is built specifically around audio. For mixed formats or social discovery, a general reading tracker may fit better.

Can one app track every listening service?

Booktrace keeps an independent record across services, but it does not automatically read or import their private histories.

Does Booktrace play audiobooks?

No. Keep listening in Audible, Libby, Spotify, Apple Books, Everand, or another player.

Is Booktrace free?

It is a free download with a trial, followed by optional subscription or lifetime purchase choices shown in the App Store.

Can I track past sessions?

Yes. Record a session afterward or use the live listening screen while it happens.

Is my history public?

No. Booktrace has no public reading profile and requires no Booktrace account.
