Built for hours, not pages.

Booktrace is a listening tracker for people whose books come through headphones — the narrators, the speed, the chapters, and the hours you actually spent.

What it does

  • Narrators as first-class. Every book carries two names, not one. Track who read what, rate the performance, and browse by narrator the same way you browse by author.
  • Speed-aware time. A 10-hour book at 1.75× is 5.7 hours of your life. Booktrace shows both the nominal runtime and your real one, on every book and every statistic.
  • Chapters, not percentages. Your place reads as “Chapter 14 of 32”, because that is where you actually are in the story.
  • Logging that survives real life. Start and stop from the lock screen, or log the whole commute once you have already parked. Nothing has to be logged live.
  • Spoiler-aware chat. Talk about a book you are halfway through without being told what happens next.

Your library stays on your device

Booktrace has no accounts and no servers. Your books, sessions, notes and history live on your phone. There is nowhere for us to store what you have been listening to, because there is no “us” to send it to.

The one real exception is the AI chat, which has to send your message somewhere to get a reply. We spell out exactly what it sends.

What it is not

Booktrace is not an audiobook player. It does not contain, stream or sell audiobooks, and it cannot read your position out of Audible, Spotify or Libby — none of them offer a way for any third-party app to do that. It keeps the record; you keep listening wherever you already do.
